Battery on the cap

The V1.20 frame has nothing that locates the battery above the step at z −15.2, and nothing stops it lifting into the roof. This redesign carries the battery in a printed cradle on the cap, closed by two screwed bars, so cap and battery come off together. Drag to orbit, scroll to zoom, right-drag to pan.

New part · pins 4–5

Two retainer bars, 2 × M2 × 6 each

Two 1.5 mm bars across the battery's front face, one low and one high, each screw into a pair of boss columns beside the cradle walls. They close the cradle into a ring, so the battery is fully captured even with the cap in your hand. The low bar clears the switch body by 0.3 mm.

Frame · pin 6, plugs

Roof openings, plug 1 on top

The stiff battery leads stand straight up through two openings in the roof that straddle the cap seam, so they never trap the cap. Plug 1 sits on top of the box and lead S comes back in through a third opening in the frame roof. The switch and DFR1202 stay as V1.20; the frame also gets 1.1 mm longer at its rim and a 1.5 mm floor recess. Plug 2 parks in the front bay.

Wiring and charging

  1. Switch up through the floor panel and the DFR1202 clipped in, exactly as V1.20. Plug 2 parks in the front bay beside the board.
  2. Feed lead S's battery end up through the frame's roof opening at the robot-right; its XT30 passes through.
  3. Battery into the cradle from the front, leads standing up either side of the middle roof tab, then both retainer bars with their four M2 × 6 screws.
  4. Offer the cap up with the leads rising through the two seam openings, fit the three cap screws, then join plug 1 on top of the box.
  5. Charging: unplug plug 1 on top, take out the three cap screws and pull the cap straight back. The battery stays captured in the cap; four bar screws release it.
